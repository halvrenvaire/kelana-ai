# Tokyo, Japan — Complete Travel Guide

## Overview
Tokyo is Japan's busy capital, mixing the ultramodern and the traditional. It is one of the world's most populous metropolises and offers an extraordinary mix of ancient temples, futuristic technology, world-class cuisine, and unique pop culture.

## Best Time to Visit
- **Spring (March–May):** Cherry blossom season (late March–early April). Most popular and crowded.
- **Autumn (September–November):** Beautiful fall foliage, comfortable temperatures.
- **Winter (December–February):** Fewer tourists, illumination festivals, skiing nearby.
- **Summer (June–August):** Hot and humid, typhoon season but vibrant festivals.

## Top Neighborhoods

### Shinjuku
- Entertainment and shopping hub
- Famous for Kabukicho nightlife, Golden Gai, Omoide Yokocho
- Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (free observation deck)
- Average accommodation: $80–$200/night (mid-range)

### Shibuya
- Fashion and youth culture center
- Famous for Shibuya Crossing, Harajuku Takeshita Street, Meiji Shrine
- Best for: shopping, people-watching, pop culture
- Average accommodation: $70–$180/night

### Asakusa
- Traditional downtown area (shitamachi)
- Famous for Senso-ji Temple, Nakamise Shopping Street, rickshaw rides
- Best for: traditional Tokyo experience, temples
- Average accommodation: $60–$150/night

### Akihabara
- Electronics and anime/manga district
- Famous for electronics shops, maid cafes, retro game stores
- Best for: tech enthusiasts, anime fans

### Ginza
- Upscale shopping and dining district
- Famous for luxury brands, art galleries, Kabuki-za Theatre
- Average accommodation: $150–$400/night (luxury)

## Budget Breakdown

### Budget Travel (USD/day)
- Accommodation: $25–$60 (capsule hotel/hostel)
- Food: $15–$30 (convenience stores, ramen shops, gyudon)
- Transport: $5–$15 (IC card/Suica)
- Activities: $10–$25 (many free temples and parks)
- **Total: ~$55–$130/day**

### Mid-Range Travel (USD/day)
- Accommodation: $80–$180 (business hotel)
- Food: $40–$80 (izakayas, sushi restaurants)
- Transport: $10–$25 (JR Pass recommended for day trips)
- Activities: $30–$60
- **Total: ~$160–$345/day**

### Luxury Travel (USD/day)
- Accommodation: $200–$800+ (5-star hotel)
- Food: $100–$300 (omakase sushi, teppanyaki)
- Transport: $30–$80 (taxi, private transfer)
- Activities: $50–$150
- **Total: ~$380–$1330/day**

## Getting Around
- **Tokyo Metro + JR Lines:** Most efficient. Buy Suica/Pasmo IC card ($5 deposit, reloadable).
- **JR Pass:** Worth it for day trips to Nikko, Kamakura, Hakone.
- **Taxi:** Expensive but convenient. Starting fare ~$7, $3–$4/km.
- **Bicycle:** Rentals available in some areas, good for flat neighborhoods.

## Must-Try Food
- **Ramen:** $8–$15, try Ichiran or Fuunji for tsukemen
- **Sushi:** Conveyor belt (kaiten) $15–$30; Tsukiji/Toyosu market for fresh sets
- **Tempura:** $10–$25 at dedicated restaurants
- **Yakitori:** $15–$30 at izakayas under railway tracks
- **Convenience Store Food:** 7-Eleven, FamilyMart — surprisingly excellent, $3–$8/meal
- **Wagyu Beef:** $50–$200+ at premium restaurants

## Day Trips from Tokyo
- **Nikko:** Ornate shrines and mausoleums, 2 hours by train (~$30 round trip)
- **Kamakura:** Giant Buddha, coastal temples, 1 hour by train (~$15 round trip)
- **Hakone:** Mt. Fuji views, hot springs (onsen), 1.5 hours (~$40 round trip)
- **Kyoto:** Ancient capital, 2.5 hours by Shinkansen (~$130 round trip)

## Visa Information
- **Visa-free:** 68 countries including US, UK, EU, Australia for up to 90 days
- **Tourist Visa:** Required for other nationalities, apply at Japanese embassy

## Practical Tips
- IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) work on most trains, buses, and convenience stores
- Most restaurants have plastic food displays or picture menus
- Tipping is NOT customary and can be considered rude
- Wi-Fi pocket routers available at airport (~$5/day)
- 7-Eleven ATMs accept international cards
- Smoking areas strictly designated — do not smoke on streets

## Cultural Etiquette
- Queue orderly everywhere — Japanese are very strict about lines
- No eating or drinking while walking (except at festivals)
- Speak quietly on public transport
- Remove shoes when entering traditional restaurants and ryokan
- Bow when greeting — deeper bow = more respect
